CHAPTER FOUR

MARKET & COMMERCE

Silk thread, handlooms, and the trade that gave this city its nickname.

THE MAIN EVENT

Tussar silk, spun by hand

Bhagalpur has been a silk-weaving town for somewhere around two centuries, and its Tussar (also spelled Tasar or Tussah) silk is famous nationwide for its natural texture, sheen, and durability. The trade runs on handlooms passed down through families — tens of thousands of weavers and around as many looms operate across the cluster, and roughly a hundred thousand people are involved in separating silk fibre from cocoon and spinning it into yarn alone.

~35,000 handloom weavers
2 million metres of silk / year
Exported to Europe, the US, Japan & West Asia

Not just silk: the Jardalu mango

Bhagalpur's other big export is the Jardalu mango, a GI-tagged (Geographical Indication) variety grown in the district and prized for its fragrance and sweetness — a reminder that the local economy runs on more than looms alone.

Where the trade happens

Retail and wholesale silk shops cluster in the city's textile markets, where saree emporiums, handloom cooperatives, and independent weaver-run stalls sell everything from Bhagalpuri sarees and stoles to plain silk yardage. Government support runs through the Weavers' Service Centre (set up in 1974) and district schemes based out of Resham Bhawan, which help weavers sell directly and skip the middlemen.

Beyond silk

Away from the looms, Bhagalpur's economy still leans heavily on agriculture — rice, wheat, maize, oilseeds, and barley from the fertile Gangetic plains — alongside rice and sugar milling, and smaller-scale trading and retail that serves the wider district as a regional commercial hub.

Challenges on the loom

The silk trade hasn't had it easy in recent decades. Power shortages, limited access to bank credit, and rising competition from newer silk hubs like Bangalore and Ahmedabad have all put pressure on Bhagalpur's weavers, many of whom rely on high-interest local credit to keep production running. It's a trade that has survived plenty of ups and downs before — and one the city is still actively trying to modernise and protect.
